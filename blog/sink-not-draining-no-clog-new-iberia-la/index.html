Sink Not Draining and No Clog: What New Iberia, LA Homes Should Check

When the trap is clean and the sink still drains slowly, the cause is usually a vent, a line blockage further down or a disposal connection.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

You cleared the trap, ran a plunger and the sink still drains slowly or gurgles. That frustrating result is common, and it usually means the problem is not at the fixture. The blockage or fault sits further down the line or in the vent that lets air in.

This guide walks through what to check, in the order that saves the most time.

Check the easy things first

A five minute checklist

  • Remove and clean the sink stopper or strainer.
  • Check the disposal, if you have one, for a jam or a partly clogged outlet.
  • Look at the dishwasher drain hose connection and the air gap, if there is one.
  • Run hot water for a minute and see if the drain speeds up.
  • Try the other sink and fixtures to see whether it is a shared problem.

Why vents matter

Drains need air behind the water, the same way a bottle needs air to pour smoothly. A vent pipe on the roof supplies it. If a bird nest, leaves or debris blocks the vent, water drains slowly and gurgles. A plumber can check the vent with a camera or by running water through it.

Partial blockages and grease

A line can be mostly open and still be slow. Grease from cooking cools and sticks to the pipe wall, narrowing it. A cable punches a hole. A jet cleans the wall. See drain cleaning and hydro jetting to understand the difference.

If a disposal is part of the problem, garbage disposal repair covers jams and connection issues.

The disposal and the dishwasher connection

In many kitchens, the dishwasher drains through the garbage disposal or through a high loop under the counter. If the disposal outlet has a knockout plug that was never removed, the dishwasher will not drain and may back up into the sink. A kinked hose or a missing high loop does the same. Check these before you call anyone.

Using a camera to see it

When the cause is not obvious, a plumber can run a small camera into the line. It shows grease, roots, a sagging section or a joint that has slipped, and it ends the guessing. If you are going to pay for a cable or jetting, the camera helps you know it will fix the problem. See sewer inspection for what that involves.

Keep a note of when the sink slows and what you were doing. Patterns, such as only after the washer runs, give the plumber a head start.

When to stop and call

If you have checked the stopper, the disposal and the dishwasher hose and the sink still drains slowly, further effort by hand is unlikely to help. A plumber can tell you whether the cause is a vent, a coated line or a deeper blockage, and can fix it with the right tool. Call sooner if sewage smells appear, if other fixtures gurgle or if water backs up in a low drain.

When a slow drain is the city's problem

If several homes on your street have slow drains or sewage is surfacing, the cause may be the public system. The City of New Iberia Wastewater Department runs about 120 miles of sewer line, 2,200 manholes and 75 pump stations, and lists emergency numbers at (337) 369-2347 on weekdays and (337) 373-3111 after 4:30 pm, on weekends and on holidays.

If only your house is affected, the cause is on your side of the property line, and the checks above apply.
