An overflowing toilet is an emergency you can partly stop yourself. Lift the tank lid, press the flapper closed and turn the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise. If water is still rising or other drains are backing up, call the 24/7 line and ask for the next open same-day appointment.

Toilet repair covers clogs that a plunger will not clear, constant running, weak flushes, leaks at the base or the tank, loose bowls and cracked porcelain. Many repairs are quick and use inexpensive parts, so it is worth finding out what has failed before deciding to replace the whole toilet.

Overflowing toilet: do this first

1

Stop the fill.

Remove the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise.

2

Do not flush again.

Flushing a blocked toilet adds more water to the bowl.

3

Contain the water.

Put towels at the base and move rugs away. Wear gloves, because the water may carry waste.

4

Check other drains.

If the tub or sink also gurgles, the main line may be blocked, which is a sewer line cleaning job.

When to replace instead of repair

A cracked bowl or tank cannot be fixed. A toilet that clogs often, flushes badly and has needed several repairs may also be worth replacing. A modern toilet uses much less water per flush, and a new install often takes less than an hour once the old one is out.

What causes a toilet to keep clogging

Wipes marketed as flushable are among the most common causes, because they do not break down like paper. A toilet that repeats clogs despite careful use can point to a partial blockage in the line behind it, so the plumber checks the line before closing the job.

A typical toilet visit

1

Look and listen.

The plumber checks the water level, flapper, fill valve and base for leaks.

2

Clear or fix.

A clog is cleared with a closet auger. A failed part is replaced.

3

Reset if needed.

If the toilet is pulled for a new wax ring, the flange is checked and the toilet reset level.

4

Test.

The toilet is flushed several times and checked for leaks at the base and supply.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet install includes removing the old unit, checking the flange and floor, setting a new wax ring or seal and connecting a new supply line and shutoff. Bring or choose a model that matches the rough-in distance from the wall, which is usually 12 inches. For other fixtures, see faucet repair and plumbing installation.

Sources behind this page

Fixture installs follow the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code, according to the Louisiana Department of Health. The flange and rough-in dimensions are set by the toilet maker's specification sheet.
