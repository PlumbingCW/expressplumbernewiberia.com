Plumbing Repair for Frozen Pipes in New Iberia, LA

A hard freeze is rare here, so few homes are ready for one. Here is how to thaw a pipe safely and when to stop and call.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

In January 2025 south Louisiana got a taste of cold it rarely sees. The National Weather Service recorded a low of 2 degrees at the New Iberia airport station, an all-time record there, and the area spent about 36 to 40 hours below freezing. Homes built for mild winters had pipes in attics, outside walls and exposed spots that were never insulated for it.

This guide explains what to do if a pipe freezes, how to thaw it safely and what to avoid. It also covers what to check after a freeze ends.

If a pipe is frozen but not burst

1

Open the faucet.

Leave the tap open so water can flow as the ice melts and relieve pressure.

2

Find the frozen section.

Look for pipes on exterior walls, in the attic or near vents. Frost on the pipe is a clue.

3

Apply gentle heat.

Use a hair dryer or warm towels. Start near the faucet and work back toward the frozen part.

4

Check for leaks.

When flow returns, inspect the pipe and fittings along the run.

What to avoid

1

No open flame.

Never use a torch, a propane heater or a lighter on a pipe. It is a fire hazard and can damage plastic and solder.

2

No boiling water on plastic.

Hot water is fine on metal pipe in small doses, but it can crack cold PVC.

3

Do not ignore a bulge.

A bulging or split pipe is already damaged. Shut off the main.

4

Do not leave it.

If you cannot reach the frozen section, call. Waiting for a thaw can turn into a flood.

If a pipe has burst

Close the main valve at once, open a low faucet to drain the lines and call a plumber. A split pipe often shows only when it thaws. Read burst pipe repair for what the repair involves.

Protecting pipes before the next freeze

Low-cost steps

  • Wrap exposed pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls with foam insulation.
  • Cover outside faucets and disconnect hoses.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • Let a faucet drip when a hard freeze is forecast, unless your water provider says to conserve.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns.

Checking the house after the thaw

The danger often arrives after the freeze, when ice melts and a split pipe starts to flow. Walk the house once temperatures rise. Look at ceilings for new stains, at walls on the north side for damp patches and at the floor near the water heater. Listen for running water with every fixture off, and check the meter for movement.

If the meter moves or you hear water you cannot find, close the main and call. Leak detection can find a split inside a wall without opening it up.

A simple freeze plan for homes that rarely need one

Because freezes are rare here, a plan on paper is better than relying on memory. Write down where the main shutoff is, where exposed pipes run and who to call. Keep pipe insulation, a flashlight and towels in one place. That small kit and a few minutes of preparation are often the difference between a short repair and a flooded house.

Insurance, photos and records

If a freeze damages your home, take photos before you clean up. Shoot the broken pipe, the wet areas and any damaged belongings. Keep the plumber's invoice, which describes the cause and the repair. Most homeowners policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe but treat slow leaks differently, so read yours or ask your agent early. Acting quickly to stop the water and dry the area also helps a claim, because policies expect you to limit further damage.

What the January 2025 freeze looked like in Iberia Parish

The National Weather Service reported snow totals of 8.0 inches at the New Iberia airport station, and an all-time low of 2 degrees there on January 22, 2025. A station five miles northwest of Jeanerette recorded 7 degrees.

The Iberia Parish president declared a State of Local Emergency effective January 17, 2025, because of extreme cold and possible precipitation. The Louisiana Water Company, which serves New Iberia and Loreauville, asked customers to use water only when absolutely necessary during the freeze and to report leaks to (337) 365-0002.
