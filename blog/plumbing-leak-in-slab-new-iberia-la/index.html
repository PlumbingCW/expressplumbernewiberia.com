Plumbing Leak in a Slab: What New Iberia, LA Homeowners Should Check First

Warm floor spots, a meter that will not stop and the sound of running water can mean a leak in the slab. This guide walks through the checks you can do yourself.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

A plumbing leak in a slab is hard to see because the pipe is buried in or under concrete. By the time water shows at the surface, it may have been escaping for weeks. The good news is that a few simple checks can tell you whether a slab leak is likely before you pay anyone to look.

This guide is for homeowners in New Iberia and the towns around it who live in slab-on-grade houses, which are common across south Louisiana. It explains the signs, a safe way to test, what to do if you find a leak and how repair options differ.

Signs that point to a slab leak

A warm patch on the floor.

Hot water lines under the slab heat the concrete above them. Bare feet notice it first.

A water bill that climbs with no new use.

A steady leak runs day and night and shows up on the next bill.

The sound of running water.

A hiss or trickle you can hear with every fixture off, especially at night.

Damp carpet or lifting flooring.

Water forced up through the slab soaks the floor above it.

A ten minute meter test

1

Stop all water use.

Close taps and make sure the washer, dishwasher and ice maker are not running.

2

Read the meter.

Open the meter box near the street and look at the small leak indicator or low-flow dial.

3

Close the house valve.

Turn off the main at the house and watch the dial again.

4

Decide what it means.

If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is outside, between the meter and the house.

A moving dial with the house valve closed points to the service line, which is covered on the water line repair page. A dial that stops after you close the valve points to the plumbing inside the house, and that includes lines under the slab.

What to do the same day

Close the main valve whenever you cannot stop the leak, and call a plumber. A slab leak can wet the soil under the foundation and, over time, undermine it. Do not cut into the floor yourself. A plumber can find the leak with acoustic and thermal tools and mark where to open the slab, which keeps the hole small.

How slab leaks are repaired

The three common approaches
Approach.What it involves.When it fits.
Spot repair.Open the slab or tunnel under it at the leak and replace the damaged section.One leak on a line that is otherwise healthy.
Reroute.Abandon the line under the slab and run new pipe through the attic or walls.Hard-to-reach lines or repeated failures.
Repipe.Replace the supply lines throughout the house.Several leaks or pipe that is aging everywhere.

The right choice depends on the pipe, the number of leaks and the house layout. A plumber should show you the findings and give you a price for each option. For the full picture, read the slab leak repair page.

Mistakes that make a slab leak worse

The most common mistake is waiting. People notice a higher bill, tell themselves it was a hot month and let it go. A slab leak does not slow down on its own, and water that escapes under a house can soften the soil that supports the foundation. The second mistake is cutting into the floor to look. Without knowing where the pipe runs, you can open a large hole and still miss the leak.

A third mistake is treating the symptom. Replacing damp carpet or lifted flooring without fixing the pipe means the new floor gets wet too. Dry the area, shut off water if you cannot stop the leak and have the line located before you spend money on finishes.

What a plumber brings to the problem

A plumber who handles slab leaks carries three main tools. Pressure testing gear isolates which line is losing water. Acoustic equipment hears the hiss of a pressurized leak through concrete. A thermal camera shows a warm hot water line. Used together, they can point to a spot within a short distance, which is what keeps the repair opening small.

What New Iberia residents should know about water leaks

The Louisiana Water Company serves the City of New Iberia, the Village of Loreauville and parts of Iberia Parish. During the January 2025 freeze it asked customers to report leaks to its customer service office right away, at (337) 365-0002.

That office handles trouble at the meter and in the street. A leak on the house side of the meter, including a slab leak, is the owner's to repair.

The City of New Iberia Permit and Inspection Department states that a permit must be obtained for any and all work, including plumbing. Before a slab is opened, call (337) 369-2354 to ask what applies at your address.
