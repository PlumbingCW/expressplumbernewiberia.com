How to Repair the Toilet: A New Iberia, LA Homeowner Guide

Many toilet problems come down to a flapper, a fill valve or a wax ring. Here is how to tell which one you have and when to stop.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

Most toilet problems come from three small parts, and two of them are inexpensive and easy to replace. The third, a wax ring, requires lifting the toilet off the floor. Knowing which part is failing saves you a trip to the store and sometimes a call to a plumber.

This guide covers the common symptoms, what causes them and which fixes are within reach of a careful homeowner. It also covers the signs that say stop and call someone.

Start by listening and looking

The flapper test

1

Add dye.

Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and do not flush.

2

Wait.

Give it ten to fifteen minutes.

3

Check the bowl.

Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking into it.

4

Replace the flapper.

Turn off the shutoff valve, flush to empty the tank, unhook the old flapper and fit a matching one.

A new flapper is a low-cost part, and the swap takes a few minutes. Make sure it matches your tank, because flappers differ in size and style.

When it is not a simple fix

Water around the base that returns.

A failed wax ring or a cracked flange. The toilet has to be lifted.

A cracked tank or bowl.

Porcelain cannot be repaired. The toilet needs replacement.

Soft or stained floor near the toilet.

A long-term leak may have rotted the subfloor.

Repeated clogs.

A partial blockage in the line behind the toilet. See below.

A toilet that keeps clogging

One toilet that clogs often can have a weak flush. If every drain in the house is slow or gurgles, the main line is partly blocked. Plungers will not fix that, and neither will a toilet auger. A camera and cable from a plumber will. See sewer line cleaning for how that goes, and toilet repair for the full service.

Replacing a fill valve

A fill valve that will not shut off, or one that hisses long after a flush, is the other common fix. Turn off the supply, flush to empty the tank and disconnect the supply line. Unscrew the nut under the tank and lift the old valve out. A new valve drops in the same hole, and most adjust to the right height with a twist. Reconnect the supply, set the water level at the mark and check for leaks.

Take a photo of the old valve before you buy a new one. Valves differ in height and in how the supply connects, and a matching unit saves a second trip.

A word on safety and water

Keep towels nearby and wear gloves when you work with a toilet that has overflowed. The water may carry waste. Wash your hands well afterward and clean the floor with a disinfectant. If a leak has soaked the floor around the base, check for softness in the boards, because a rotted subfloor needs repair before a toilet is set back.

Toilets, wipes and the New Iberia sewer system

The City of New Iberia Wastewater Department serves more than 33,000 city users and about 1,500 parish users through two treatment plants and 75 pump stations. Wipes and rags can foul pumps and clog lines at those stations.

Products sold as flushable do not break down like toilet paper. If you want to protect your own line and the city's pumps, put wipes in the trash. For sewer emergencies outside your own house, the department lists (337) 369-2347 on weekdays and (337) 373-3111 after hours.
