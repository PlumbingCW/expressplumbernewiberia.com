A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete floor. It can start small and spread, wetting flooring, raising the water bill and in time undermining the soil under the house. If you hear running water with everything off or the floor is warm underfoot, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line.

Slab leak repair starts with finding the exact spot, so the plumber can choose between a small spot repair, rerouting the line through the attic or walls and repiping. The right option depends on the pipe, how many leaks there are and how the house is built.

Signs of a slab leak

Warm or hot spots on the floor.

A leak in a hot water line heats the concrete above it.

The sound of running water.

A hiss or trickle when every fixture is off.

A meter that keeps moving.

With the main valve at the house closed, the meter still shows flow.

Damp carpet, lifted flooring or cracks.

Water pushing up through the slab leaves visible damage.

Why slab leaks happen

Copper pipe under a slab can wear through from the outside as water moves through the surrounding soil or concrete. Pipes can also rub against the slab as the house settles, and joints made under the slab during construction can fail. In Louisiana's wet climate, constantly damp soil around the pipe adds to the wear.

Repair options compared

Spot repair, reroute or repipe
Option.Best when.Trade-off.
Tunnel or jackhammer spot repair.One leak on an otherwise healthy line.Opens the floor or tunnels under it, then patches the slab.
Reroute the line.The leak is in a hard-to-reach run, or the line has a history of failure.New pipe goes through the attic or walls, and the old line is abandoned.
Repipe.Multiple leaks or pipe known to be aging.Larger job, but replaces the failing pipe throughout, see repiping.

The plumber explains which option makes sense after the leak is located. A single leak on a young line is usually a spot repair. A second or third leak on the same line within a few years points toward a reroute or repipe.

Act on slab leaks quickly

Concrete can hide a leak for months, and the soil beneath can erode as water escapes. If you see damp flooring, a growing water bill or a warm floor patch, close the main valve and have the line checked. Waiting turns a small repair into a larger one and can lead to mold under flooring. For the diagnostic side, see leak detection.

Insurance and documentation

Slab leaks can trigger a homeowners claim, and many policies treat sudden damage differently from long-term seepage. Ask your agent before work starts, take photos of damage and keep the plumber's written findings. Our visit includes an explanation of what was found and where, in writing if you request it.
