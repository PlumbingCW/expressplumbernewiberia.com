How to Repair a Leaky Outdoor Faucet Handle: A New Iberia, LA Homeowner Guide

Water around the handle of a hose bib usually means packing, not a failed valve. It is a ten minute repair.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

An outdoor faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, takes more abuse than any fixture in the house. It sits in sun, rain and humidity, and it is turned on and off with the hose still attached. A leak around the handle is common and usually easy to fix.

This guide covers the common leaks, how to fix a handle leak and how to tell when the problem is bigger than a spigot.

Where the water comes from

What each leak means
Where it leaks.Likely cause.Fix.

Fixing a handle leak

1

Shut off the supply.

If there is no indoor shutoff, close the main valve at the house.

2

Try the packing nut.

With the faucet on, tighten the nut behind the handle a quarter turn and see if the leak stops.

3

Replace the packing.

If it still leaks, remove the handle and nut, and replace the packing washer or string.

4

Reassemble and test.

Turn the water on slowly and check for leaks at the handle and spout.

A packing leak that has gone on for months can have stained the wall behind it. If the siding or trim looks wet, ask a plumber to check for water in the wall.

After a freeze

Outdoor faucets are the most exposed to freeze damage because water inside the valve and the pipe behind it can freeze and split the pipe inside the wall. The split often shows only after a thaw, as water inside the wall or a spray when you turn on the tap. A frost-proof sillcock has a long stem so the valve sits inside the warm part of the wall. See burst pipe repair for what a split pipe repair involves.

Upgrade if it keeps failing

A new frost-proof sillcock with a vacuum breaker is a simple replacement. A plumber can also add an isolation valve inside so you can shut off one outdoor faucet without shutting off the house. See faucet repair for related work.

Hoses left on and why they matter

A hose left attached traps water in the faucet and can add stress to the stem. In heat, the trapped water softens seals. In cold, it freezes and splits the faucet body. Make it a habit to disconnect and drain the hose after use, and use a hose washer that is in good shape.

A vacuum breaker on the faucet keeps dirty water from flowing back into the house supply, so if yours is missing or cracked, replace it.

A quick monthly check

Once a month, walk around the house and open each outdoor faucet. Look for drips at the handle, at the spout and at the wall. A small drip is easy to fix and costs little. A wet wall that has been dripping for months can rot sheathing, so catching it early is worthwhile.

When a plumber is the better choice

If the faucet is soldered to a copper pipe inside the wall, replacing it takes torch work or compression fittings, and a mistake can leave a leak inside the wall. A plumber has the tools to cut and sweat the joint safely and can add an isolation valve at the same time. That is also the right moment to switch to a frost-proof model if you do not already have one.

Freezes and hose bibs in Iberia Parish

During the January 2025 winter storm, the National Weather Service recorded a low of 2 degrees at the New Iberia airport station and about 36 to 40 hours below freezing. The Louisiana Water Company asked customers to report leaks right away at (337) 365-0002.

Hose bibs that were not covered or disconnected were among the likely casualties. After any hard freeze, turn each outdoor tap on slowly and look for water inside the wall or at the base.

Safety: when to call right away

If water is spraying from a hose bib and will not stop, close the main valve and call right away. Do not force a seized handle.
