A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so the hot water does not run out during a long shower. It also takes up far less space than a tank. The trade-off is a more demanding install, because the unit needs a bigger gas line or a heavy electrical circuit and a vent to match.

Our tankless service covers new installs, conversions from a tank, repairs for error codes and cold water sandwiches, and yearly flushing to remove scale. Call during the day to schedule, and ask for the next appointment that fits your week.

Is tankless right for your home

Tank versus tankless
Question.Tank.Tankless.
Hot water supply.Limited by tank size.Continuous while the unit keeps up with flow.
Space.Needs floor space.Wall mounted, much smaller.
Install work.Usually a straight swap.Larger gas line or electrical upgrade, new vent.
Maintenance.Flush the tank, check the anode.Annual flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger.

A tankless heater has a maximum flow rate, so running two showers and a dishwasher together can overtax a small unit. The plumber adds up your fixtures and recommends a unit that matches the highest flow you will need at once.

Why scale matters here

Water with a high mineral content leaves scale inside a tankless unit's heat exchanger, which cuts flow and can trigger error codes. The hardness of the water in New Iberia is a number the water utility can tell you, and we do not guess at it here. Ask for the current figure, because it decides how often a descaling flush is worth doing.

Common tankless problems

Error codes on the display.

Many codes point to scale, a blocked vent or low gas pressure.

Cold water sandwich.

Hot, then cold, then hot again when flow changes. Often a sensor or flow issue.

Low flow of hot water.

Scale on the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet filter.

Unit cycles on and off.

A flow that is too low to keep the unit lit, or an undersized supply.

Installing a tankless unit

1

Check the gas or power.

The plumber measures gas line size and pressure or the electrical capacity at the panel.

2

Plan the vent.

Tankless units use a specific vent material and path, which may be new.

3

Mount and connect.

The unit is mounted, connected with isolation valves and set up for flushing.

4

Commission.

The plumber programs the temperature, checks flow and reviews the controls with you.

Flushing and descaling

A flush runs a descaling solution through the heat exchanger and then rinses it out. It is the single most useful bit of maintenance on a tankless unit. Ask when the unit was last flushed if you bought a home with one already in place.

If you are weighing a tankless against a standard tank, read water heater installation for the tank side. If your current unit has failed completely, water heater replacement covers the fast swap.

Sources behind this page

Tankless units are installed to the maker's instructions and the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code, according to the Louisiana Department of Health. The installation manual sets vent material, length and clearances.

Safety: when to call right away

If you smell gas near a tankless unit, leave the house first and call your gas utility from outside. Do not operate the unit.
