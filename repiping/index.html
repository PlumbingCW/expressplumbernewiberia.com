Repiping replaces the supply pipes that carry water through your house with new ones. It is a planned project, not an emergency fix, and the right time to call is when repairs on the same system keep adding up. We walk the house, show you the pipe and give you a written price before any wall is opened.

Most repipes are done to stop repeat leaks, restore water pressure or remove pipe that is at the end of its life. The work is done room by room, with water shut off for part of each day, and walls are opened only where access is needed.

Signs your house may need repiping

Repeated pinhole leaks.

A second or third leak in the same line points to pipe-wide corrosion.

Falling water pressure.

Mineral buildup and corrosion narrow old pipe from the inside.

Brown or rusty water.

Corroding galvanized or iron pipe sheds rust.

Visible green or white crust.

Corrosion on copper joints or fittings.

Pipe materials compared

Common replacement choices
Material.Strengths.Keep in mind.
PEX.Flexible, fewer joints and quick to install.Must be kept out of direct sun and has its own fittings.
Copper.Long track record and heat tolerance.Costs more, and some water chemistry can wear it from the inside.
CPVC.Does not corrode and uses solvent-weld joints.Can become brittle with age and heat.

The plumber recommends a material after looking at your house, the water chemistry and local code. Water hardness in New Iberia is a figure your water provider can give you, and it matters for choosing pipe and for deciding whether a scale control makes sense.

Old houses and newer ones

Houses in the East Main Street Historic District date from 1834 to 1933 and may have galvanized or other older supply pipe, if the plumbing was never fully updated. Mid-century homes elsewhere in Iberia Parish may have galvanized steel or early copper. Each pipe type has its own age pattern, which is why a plumber looks at the pipe itself, not just the date on the house.

How a repipe is done

1

Walk-through.

The plumber traces the lines, checks the water heater and shutoffs and counts fixtures.

2

Written price.

You see the scope, the material and the price in writing before the work is scheduled.

3

Replacement.

New lines are run through attics, walls and crawlspaces, with the old lines capped or removed.

4

Test and patch.

The system is pressure tested, and wall openings are closed or left for a drywall contractor.

Planning around the work

Water is off for part of each day while connections are made. Plan to be home for the first day, set aside bottled water and clear the areas where walls will be opened. The plumber explains the schedule and which rooms are affected before work begins.

If you only have one leak, leak detection and a spot repair may be all you need. For a pipe that has already burst, see burst pipe repair.

Sources behind this page

Replacing supply lines follows the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code, according to the Louisiana Department of Health. The City of New Iberia Permit and Inspection Department states that a permit is required for any and all work, including plumbing.

What affects the price of repiping

Repiping is quoted after the walk-through. These factors have the largest effect.

Size of the house.More fixtures and longer runs need more pipe.
Pipe material.PEX, copper and CPVC differ in material and labor.
Access.An open attic and crawlspace are easier than a slab house with interior walls.
Drywall and paint.Patching and finish work may be separate.
Permits.Where a permit is required, fees and inspection timing apply.

Repiping for worn-out pipe is usually a maintenance cost, not an insurance claim. If a sudden leak damaged the house, your policy may cover resulting damage.

Safety: when to call right away

If a supply line is leaking inside a wall, close the main valve and call right away. Do not cut into drywall near wiring.
