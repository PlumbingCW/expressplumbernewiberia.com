A sewer line camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible cable and runs it through your drain line while a monitor shows the pipe in real time. It is the only way to see roots, cracks, sags and offset joints without digging. The visit can be scheduled at a time that works for you, with same-day appointments when available.

The footage turns guesses into decisions. Instead of clearing the same clog every few months, you see what is holding it in place and whether a spot repair, lining or a simple cleaning makes sense.

When a camera inspection is worth booking

Good reasons to look inside the line

  • You are buying an older home and want to know what the drain line looks like before closing.
  • A drain has backed up more than once and cable cleaning has not stopped it.
  • A yard has a soft, sunken patch that stays wet without rain.
  • You plan to add a bathroom or a kitchen and want to know the existing line can handle it.
  • A contractor has recommended replacing the sewer line and you want a second opinion with video.

What the camera shows

Roots.

Fine roots at a joint look like white threads. Heavy roots fill the pipe like a rope.

Cracks and breaks.

A crack shows as a dark line. A break shows soil or open space at the pipe wall.

Sags, called bellies.

Water pools in a low spot and leaves solids behind, which repeats the clog.

Offset joints.

Pipe sections that have shifted so the inside edge catches paper.

Older homes, newer homes and what to expect

New Iberia was founded in 1779, and the East Main Street Historic District lists buildings from 1834 to 1933. Pipe from that era is a different animal from the plastic used in newer subdivisions. For an older house, expect the camera to show material that has aged, and plan questions about whether any sections have been repaired before.

In newer homes, camera inspections often catch workmanship issues such as sags at the connection to the main. Catching one before the warranty on a house expires is cheaper than finding it after the first backup.

A note for buyers and sellers

A seller who has the line inspected before listing can answer a buyer's questions with footage instead of promises. A buyer who inspects before closing can use the findings to ask for repairs or a price adjustment. Neither a cleaning nor a camera pass is a guarantee about the future, and the report describes the line on the day it was inspected.
