Using a Plumbing Snake on Clogged Drains: A New Iberia, LA Guide

A hand snake is a good tool for a simple clog. It is the wrong tool for a coated line or a main line backup.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

A drain snake, also called an auger, is a flexible cable that you feed into a drain to break up or hook a clog. For a bathroom sink or tub with hair and soap in the trap, it works well. For a main line or a grease-coated kitchen drain, it often fails, and forcing it can make things worse.

This guide shows when to reach for a snake, how to use one and when to stop.

Good jobs for a hand snake

Hair in a tub or shower.

A short cable pulls out the wad of hair that holds soap.

A slow bathroom sink.

Hair and soap collect in the trap and the first bend.

A clogged toilet.

A closet auger, a short version with a protected tip, is made for this.

A small kitchen blockage close to the trap.

A short cable can clear food residue near the fixture.

How to use one

1

Prepare.

Wear gloves, place a bucket and towels and remove the stopper or strainer.

2

Feed the cable.

Push it in gently until you feel resistance, then tighten the thumbscrew.

3

Turn the handle.

Crank to work the tip into the clog, then push and pull to break it up.

4

Withdraw and flush.

Pull the cable out slowly, clean it and run hot water for several minutes.

When to stop

1

The cable will not go farther.

You may have reached a bend, a joint or a hard blockage. Forcing it can scratch or puncture pipe.

2

More than one drain is slow.

The blockage is in a shared branch or the main.

3

Water comes back after clearing.

The clog is returning because of buildup on the pipe wall.

4

A smell of sewage.

Do not keep working. Call a plumber.

Call and describe what you tried. A plumber can tell you what is likely next.

For repeat clogs, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall instead of poking a hole. For one slow drain, drain cleaning is the standard service.

Choosing a snake for home use

A hand-cranked auger with a 25 foot cable fits most home needs. Shorter tools such as a drum auger suit sinks and tubs, while a closet auger is the right tool for toilets. Electric snakes for home use are available, but they can twist and kink a cable if used carelessly, and they can scar old pipe.

Look for a cable with a protective coating if you work on porcelain, and keep the cable clean after each use. A rusty cable can scratch pipe and carry bacteria.

Preventing the next clog

Habits that help

  • Put a hair catcher in every shower and tub drain.
  • Scrape plates into the trash before rinsing them.
  • Pour grease into a can, not down the sink.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes.
  • Keep wipes, cotton and paper towels out of toilets.

A note on chemical drain cleaners

Liquid openers are tempting, but they do not clear hair well, and they can damage old pipe and fixtures. They also make later work riskier, since a plumber who opens a line full of caustic liquid has to take care to avoid splashes. If a clog is stubborn, a mechanical method is safer for you and for the pipe. If you have already used a chemical product, tell whoever does the follow-up work so they can wear the right protection.

If you are unsure what you are dealing with, take a photo of the drain and note which fixtures are affected. That short record helps a plumber decide quickly whether a hand snake, a motorized cable or a camera is the right place to start, and it saves time on the phone.

Know your sewer responsibility in New Iberia

If a drain backs up in your house but the street seems fine, the problem is your line. The City of New Iberia Wastewater Department lists emergency numbers for the public system at (337) 369-2347 on weekdays and (337) 373-3111 after 4:30 pm, on weekends and holidays.

Call them if sewage is surfacing in the street or several neighbors are backing up at once.
