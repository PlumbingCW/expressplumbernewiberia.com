Delcambre sits at an elevation of about 7 feet beside the Delcambre Canal, also known as Bayou Carlin, with Lake Peigneur roughly a mile to the north. That low ground shapes its plumbing. Drains here move water more slowly after a heavy rain, and sewer lines can back up when the ground around them is saturated. Our emergency line is open 24 hours a day.

The canal, the lake and the water table

The town straddles the border of Vermilion and Iberia parishes, and the canal that runs through it carries much of the local fishing industry. Near the water, soil stays wet for long stretches, and pipes in wet soil corrode faster. Cleanout caps can also sit under standing water, which makes a blocked sewer line harder to reach.

If a toilet and tub back up together after a rain, the main line is the likely cause. See sewer line cleaning for how that works, and ask the plumber to check for roots or a sag in the line.

What Hurricane Rita taught Delcambre

In 2005, storm surge from Hurricane Rita put water as deep as six feet in parts of town, and a report from the time said only 25 of about 900 homes escaped flooding. People here know what water in a house means. Plumbing that sat under floodwater, including water heaters, gas controls and drain lines, needs to be checked before it is switched back on.

Raising the heater

A water heater set on a raised platform keeps its burner and controls farther from rising water. That is a cheap change when a unit is being replaced anyway. Read about water heater replacement and ask whether a platform fits in your garage or closet.

Water in Delcambre

The Town of Delcambre Water System draws groundwater and, according to federal records, serves about 2,772 people. We have not found a published hardness figure, so we do not give one. Ask the town for its latest water quality report, and ask what the hardness number is before buying a softener.

Fishing docks, shops and boat sheds

Businesses along the canal and in town include fish houses, boat shops and seafood sellers, and their drains see scraps, scales and grease. A kitchen or wash-down line that serves a seafood business clogs differently than a house drain. If you run one, see commercial plumbing and ask about hydro jetting on a schedule.
