Hurricane season and what it does to plumbing

The eye of Hurricane Andrew passed directly over Loreauville in 1992, and residents were without electric power for more than four weeks. A long outage, even a shorter one, leaves plumbing stressed. Water heaters sit cold, sump pumps stop and sewer lines can back up where pumps lose power.

After a storm, check for new leaks before you turn everything back on. Listen for running water with all fixtures off, and look at the meter. If it still moves, call and ask about leak detection.

Winter freezes are rare and costly

In January 2025 the Louisiana Water Company, which serves New Iberia, Loreauville and parts of Iberia Parish, asked customers to conserve water at the height of a hard freeze and to report leaks quickly. Pipes that split in attics and outside walls were the cause of many calls across the area. A plumber can repair the split section and check the rest of the run for cracks.

Boats, sugarcane and a small village's plumbing

Sugarcane farming and boat building are important to Loreauville's economy, and many properties include outbuildings, workshops and shops with their own water lines. A line run to a barn or boat shed is easy to forget and slow to be noticed when it leaks. Ask for a leak check if the water bill rises with no change in household use.

Properties in the village and around Lake Fausse Pointe vary in age and layout. A plumber looks at the line from the meter to each building and explains where the weak points are. For problems between the meter and the house, see water line repair.
