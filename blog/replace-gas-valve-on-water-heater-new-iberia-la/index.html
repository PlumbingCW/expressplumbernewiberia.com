Replace the Gas Valve on a Water Heater: A New Iberia, LA Homeowner Guide

The gas control valve decides whether the burner lights. Here is how to tell it has failed and why replacement belongs to a licensed plumber.

Plumber on call.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

The gas control valve on a water heater is the part that lights the pilot, senses the temperature and opens the gas to the burner. When it fails, the heater stops making hot water and may leak gas. Because this part controls fuel, replacing it is not a good do-it-yourself job.

This guide explains what the valve does, how to tell it is the problem and what to do. It also covers the safety steps that matter more than any repair tip.

Safety first

1

If you smell gas.

Leave the house, do not use switches or phones inside and call your gas utility from outside.

2

Turn the control to off.

Only if you can do it safely and there is no smell of gas.

3

Do not relight repeatedly.

A pilot that goes out again and again is a symptom, not something to keep fixing.

4

Call a plumber.

Describe the symptom. A plumber tests the parts in order.

What the gas valve does

The valve holds the thermostat, the pilot control and the main gas shutoff in one body. A thermocouple or flame sensor tells it the pilot is lit. When the sensor reports no flame, the valve closes the gas. When the water cools below the setpoint, the valve opens the gas to the burner.

Signs of a failed valve

What you see and what it may mean
Symptom.Possible cause.Next step.
Pilot will not light.Failed igniter, valve or no gas supply.Plumber tests in order.
Pilot lights but goes out.Bad thermocouple or flame sensor, or a weak valve.Replace the sensor first, then test the valve.
Water is cold, pilot is lit.Valve does not open to the burner.Test the valve, replace if it fails.
Water gets too hot.Thermostat in the valve is stuck.Turn the heater off and call.

A thermocouple is a cheaper part than a valve, so a plumber checks it before blaming the valve. If the valve is bad, the replacement is a matched part that fits the make and model.

Repair or replace the heater

A valve on a young heater is worth replacing. On a unit near ten years old, the cost of the valve plus the risk of a tank leak can tip the balance toward a new heater. The plumber reads the manufacture date and explains both costs. See water heater repair and water heater replacement.

What a plumber checks before replacing a valve

A careful plumber does not start by replacing the most expensive part. The usual order is to check that gas reaches the heater, that the pilot orifice and tube are clean, that the thermocouple or flame sensor works and that the vent is drawing properly. If those pass and the valve still will not behave, the valve is the likely fault.

After a replacement, the plumber tests for gas leaks at every joint that was touched, usually with a leak detection solution, and checks the flame and the draft. Those steps are why the job is done by a licensed professional.

Signs a heater is not worth a new valve

If the tank shows rust at the base, water on the floor or a manufacture date more than about ten years back, a new valve may only delay a replacement. A plumber can read the date on the rating label and tell you how old the unit is. When a heater is that age, many owners decide to replace the whole unit instead.

Licensing and codes for gas work in Louisiana

Plumbers in Louisiana are licensed by the State Plumbing Board of Louisiana, which lists its website as spbla.com. You can check a plumber's license there before work begins.

According to the Louisiana Department of Health, plumbing requirements are now published as part of the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code, set by the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code Council. For a gas water heater in New Iberia, the city Permit and Inspection Department at (337) 369-2354 can tell you what applies.
