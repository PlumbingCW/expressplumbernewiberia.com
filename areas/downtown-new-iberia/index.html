Commercial Plumber in Downtown New Iberia, LA

Plumbing for restaurants, shops and offices in the downtown historic commercial district, from grease lines and floor drains to leaks and permits.

This page is about commercial and mixed-use plumbing, not a residential neighborhood. Downtown New Iberia is a National Register of Historic Places district, listed on December 13, 2017 as the Downtown New Iberia Commercial Historic District.

According to the listing, the district is roughly bounded by Fulton, Burke, Weeks, St. Peter and Jefferson Streets and covers about 69 acres. It holds 121 buildings, 73 of them contributing, with dates from 1870 to 1967. Its contributing properties include stores, offices, a hotel, a theater, a service station and a museum.

Plumbing for restaurants, shops and offices

A downtown building can be a restaurant on the ground floor and offices above, with lines shared between tenants. The first questions are which unit is affected, who owns the lines and who approves repairs. A tenant usually tells the building owner or manager before calling a plumber.

For general commercial work, see commercial plumbing. Kitchens add their own needs, from grease lines to floor drains and water heaters sized for heavy use.

Grease lines and floor drains

Fats and oils cool and harden inside drain lines, so a kitchen line carries a heavier load than a house line. Cleaning a line on a schedule is usually cheaper than clearing a full blockage.

Ask the City of New Iberia Wastewater Department, at (337) 369-2347, what rules apply to grease from your kitchen before you set a cleaning routine.

Hydro jetting scours the inside of a line with high-pressure water, and a camera check shows whether a recurring blockage is grease, roots or a damaged pipe. A sewer camera inspection is also useful before buying or leasing an older building.

Older commercial buildings

Buildings in the district date from 1870 to 1967, and many have been altered for new uses. That makes the actual pipe more important than the date. Leak detection helps find a hidden leak in a shared wall or ceiling without opening large areas.

Before you call about a commercial space, note

  • The address, the unit and whether lines are shared with other tenants.
  • Whether the problem affects one fixture, one floor or the whole building.
  • Whether food service, floor drains or grease lines are involved.
  • Who owns the building and who approves repair work.
  • Whether you need the work done outside business hours, and ask if that is available.

Permits and review

Commercial work usually needs a permit, and the city states that one is required for any and all work. Phone the Permit and Inspection Department, (337) 369-2354, before work begins. The city also keeps a Historic Downtown section, so Planning and Zoning at (337) 369-2330 can say whether exterior changes need review.
