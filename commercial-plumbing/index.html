When a restaurant floor drain backs up before the dinner rush or a restroom floods in a shop, the cost of waiting is lost business. The 24/7 line takes commercial calls any hour. Commercial plumbing covers drains, grease lines, restrooms, water heaters, backflow devices, water lines and fixtures in non-residential buildings. The pipe is bigger, the use is heavier and the downtime matters more, so the plumber works to get you open again and then explains what should be fixed for good.

A commercial emergency: first steps

1

Protect people.

Close off wet areas and put out signs. Wet floors in a public space are a hazard.

2

Stop the water.

Find the main shutoff for the building or the affected area, and close it.

3

Call the 24/7 line.

Say the type of business, the symptom and any alarms or equipment affected.

4

Notify the landlord.

If you lease, tell the property owner or manager so that responsibility is clear.
