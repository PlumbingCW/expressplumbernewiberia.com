A disposal that hums and will not spin has a jam. One that is leaking under the sink can flood a cabinet. Switch the disposal off at the wall, keep your hand out of the drain and call the 24/7 line if water is spreading. Many disposal repairs take less than an hour.

Garbage disposal repair covers jams, tripped reset buttons, leaks at the sink flange or the drain connection, dead motors and slow drains caused by the unit. If a disposal is past saving, the plumber replaces it with a new one that fits your sink.

Disposal not working: safe first steps

1

Turn it off and unplug it.

Switch the wall switch off. If the unit plugs in under the sink, unplug it before you touch anything.

2

Never use your hand.

Use tongs or pliers to remove an object you can see, never fingers.

3

Use the hex key.

Most disposals have a hex socket on the bottom. Turning it by hand frees a stuck flywheel.

4

Press reset.

The red reset button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats. Wait a few minutes, then press it.

What should not go down

Grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice, potato peels and fibrous scraps such as celery and corn husks are common culprits. They do not always jam the disposal, but they gather in the line below it. Local kitchens produce plenty of both, from crawfish boils to rice dishes, and the kitchen line is often the one that clogs. If the sink backs up, see drain cleaning.

Choosing a replacement

Disposals are rated by horsepower. A half horsepower unit handles light use, and three quarters to one horsepower suits a busy kitchen. Check that the new unit fits under your sink and works with your dishwasher connection. The plumber matches the mount and the discharge to the sink you have.

If a leak under the sink is spreading beyond the disposal, leak detection can find where it starts. For new fixtures in a remodel, see plumbing installation.
