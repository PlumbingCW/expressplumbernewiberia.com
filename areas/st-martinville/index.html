St. Martinville is the seat of St. Martin Parish, about ten miles northeast of New Iberia along Bayou Teche, and it has some of the oldest buildings in the region. Old buildings mean old pipe. Our emergency line is open 24 hours a day, and calls from the city are dispatched from New Iberia.

Petit Paris and its aging plumbing

The city earned the nickname Petit Paris, or Little Paris, when it was a cultural center with fine hotels and the Duchamp Opera House, which opened in 1830. Landmarks such as the Evangeline Oak and the Church of St. Martin of Tours anchor the downtown. Houses and commercial buildings from that era and later can still carry iron, galvanized or early copper pipe.

Those pipes corrode from the inside and leak at joints. The signs are rust colored water, low pressure and a pinhole leak that returns after repair. A repiping walk-through shows what the pipe really is and whether a replacement makes sense.

Water in St. Martinville

The City of St. Martinville Water System draws groundwater from two wells. Its 2025 report lists treated hardness at 16.3 mg/L, which is soft, and a pH of 7.05. Soft water leaves little scale in heaters and fixtures, so mineral buildup is not the usual culprit here. When a heater fails in St. Martinville it is more often age or a corroded anode than sediment.

The same report lists sodium at 184 mg/L and chloride at 118 mg/L. Neither is a plumbing emergency, but a plumber may mention them when talking about fixture wear. For details, read the city's report or ask its water office. See water heater repair for how those jobs go.

Snow, frost and old walls

The January 2025 storm dropped about 8.1 inches of snow at a station in St. Martinville. Old buildings with thin wall cavities and uninsulated pipe saw the worst of it. After a freeze, check for stains on ceilings and for damp spots near exterior walls. If you find any, leak detection can locate a split without opening the whole wall.

A different parish, a different office

St. Martinville is in St. Martin Parish, not Iberia Parish, so permit and inspection questions go to a different office than in New Iberia. Call the city or the parish before you start work, and ask what applies to your address. We do not assume New Iberia's rules apply here.
