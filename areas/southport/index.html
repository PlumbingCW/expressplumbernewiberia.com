Plumbing for different kinds of housing

A subdivision with several housing types has several plumbing situations. The right first call depends on whether you own the home, rent it or live in an apartment building.

Who to call first

If you own a house or mobile home, the lines from the water meter onward are yours. Close the main valve if water is flowing, then call a plumber.

If you rent, tell the landlord or manager first, because repairs and plumber choice are usually theirs to decide. Take photos and note the time of any damage.

In many manufactured homes the supply and drain lines run under the floor, often behind skirting or an access panel. That can make a leak harder to see, so a damp floor or a soft spot deserves a prompt look. Leak detection can find the source before anything is cut open.

Common calls anywhere include a backed-up drain, a failed water heater and a running toilet. Drain cleaning, water heater repair and toilet repair cover the everyday ones. A line that is damaged under the yard is a water line repair job.

After storm damage

The National Weather Service rated the December 14, 2022 tornado that struck New Iberia a top-end EF-2. Plumbing in any storm-damaged building should be checked before it is used again. Close the main valve if lines may be broken, stay away from outlets near water, and ask a plumber to test the system.

Insurance may cover water damage from a sudden failure even when the pipe or heater itself is not covered. Call your agent early and photograph the damage before cleanup.

If water is running or backing up

  • Close the main water valve and keep people away from standing water near outlets.
  • If sewage is rising, stop using all water in the building.
  • If you smell gas near a gas water heater, leave first and call your gas utility from outside.
  • Move belongings off the floor and take photos for insurance.

Public sewer or your own line

A blocked drain in an apartment or a house does not always start inside. Wastewater leaves through a private line and then joins the public main, which the city maintains. If neighbors back up at once, call the city sewer numbers, (337) 369-2347 or (337) 373-3111. If the trouble is limited to one unit or one house, the blockage is usually in the private line.
