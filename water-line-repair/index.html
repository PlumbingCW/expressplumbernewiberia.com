The water line between the meter and your house is yours to maintain. When it breaks, water either floods the yard or pressure collapses inside the house. If you see a geyser or a growing wet patch, close the valve at the house and, if you can reach it, the one at the meter, then call the 24/7 line.

Water line repair covers a broken section of the service line, a failed fitting at the meter or house, and full replacement of an aging line. The plumber finds the break, explains whether a spot repair or a replacement makes sense and prices both before digging starts.

Signs of a service line problem

A wet or sunken yard strip.

Soft ground between the meter and the house that does not dry.

Low water pressure everywhere.

A leak or a corroded line limits flow to the whole house.

A meter running with the house shut off.

The leak indicator moves with every fixture closed.

Discolored or gritty water.

Corrosion or a broken line can let debris into the pipe.

Who owns which part

The meter is usually where the water provider's responsibility ends and yours begins. A leak at the meter itself or in the street belongs to the provider, which is the party named on your water bill. A leak on the house side of the meter is a private plumbing repair. If you are not sure, the plumber can tell you after a pressure test, and you call the provider if the problem is theirs.

Before anyone digs

Louisiana requires notice before excavation so that buried utilities are marked. The state one-call center, Louisiana 811, handles those requests, and the plumber will place or confirm the request before any machine goes in the ground. If you plan your own digging, call 811 first.

A suspected leak that has not been located is a job for leak detection. If the pipe inside the house is the problem, see burst pipe repair.

Excavation requires a locate request through Louisiana 811 first, according to the state's one-call program. Plumbers are licensed by the State Plumbing Board of Louisiana, which lists its site as spbla.com.
