The East Main Street Historic District runs along East Main Street from Weeks Street to Philip Street, and its listing also includes Lee Street and Ann Street. It is a National Register of Historic Places district, listed on July 28, 1983, and it covers about 30 acres of New Iberia beside Bayou Teche.

According to the National Register listing, the district holds 71 buildings, 57 of them contributing, with construction dates from 1834 to 1933. Shadows-on-the-Teche at 317 East Main Street, built in 1834, is a National Historic Landmark. The Steamboat House at 623 East Main Street dates from 1882.

What a historic district means for plumbing

A district like this mixes houses with civic and commercial buildings from several eras, so no single date describes the plumbing. A building that is 90 or 190 years old has usually been repaired, added to and rerouted many times. Its address does not tell a plumber what is behind the walls, and this page does not guess.

That is why a plumber should look at the actual pipe before recommending anything. A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of a drain line, and leak detection can locate a hidden leak without opening finished walls first.

Ask before you change the outside of a building

The City of New Iberia Planning and Zoning page lists Historic District meeting agendas and minutes. If a project would change the outside of a building, such as a new vent termination, an exterior cleanout or a moved meter, ask Planning and Zoning at (337) 369-2330 whether review applies.

Plumbing permits go through the city's Permit and Inspection Department at (337) 369-2354. The city says a permit must be obtained for any and all work, including plumbing.

Common plumbing questions in older buildings

This is general plumbing information, not a claim about how often any problem occurs on East Main. A slow drain in an older building can come from a blocked fixture trap or from the line behind it, and the fix is different. Drain cleaning clears a blockage, and a camera shows whether the line itself is damaged.

Supply pipe matters too. If water is rusty, pressure has dropped or leaks keep returning on different sections, ask a plumber about the material and age of the lines. Where much of the system has been replaced already, repiping may only be needed in the remaining old sections.

Before you call about an older building, note

  • Where the main water shutoff is, and whether you can turn it.
  • Which fixtures are affected and when the problem started.
  • Any past renovations or pipe repairs you know of.
  • Whether you own the building or rent, and who manages repairs.
  • Whether any water has reached electrical outlets or fixtures.

Street sewer or your own line

According to the City of New Iberia Wastewater Department, the public system includes 120 miles of sewer line, 2,200 manholes and 75 pump stations. If sewage backs up in several buildings at once, the public main may be involved. The city's sewer numbers are (337) 369-2347 and (337) 373-3111. If only your building is affected, the problem is usually on your own side of the property line.
