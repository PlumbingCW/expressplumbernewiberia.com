Plumbing installation is planned work. It is what happens when you add a bathroom, rebuild a kitchen, replace worn fixtures or finish a space that has no water yet. Call during the day to schedule a visit, and the plumber will measure, look at what is already in the walls and give you the price in writing.

Our installation work covers sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers, dishwashers, ice makers, washing machine boxes, outdoor spigots and the supply and drain lines that serve them. We also do rough-in plumbing for additions, which is the piping that goes in before the walls are closed.

What a good plan covers

Questions to answer before work begins

  • Where will each fixture go, and how close is the nearest supply and drain?
  • Can the existing drain handle added fixtures, or does a new line need to be run?
  • Is the water heater large enough for the added demand?
  • Does the work need a permit, and who will pull it?
  • When will walls and floors be open, and what is the schedule for the other trades?

Permits and rules in New Iberia

The City of New Iberia Permit and Inspection Department says a permit must be obtained for any and all work, including plumbing. Call (337) 369-2354 before you start so you know what applies at your address, especially if you are outside the city limits. Plumbing requirements are published as part of the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code, which the Louisiana Department of Health references.

Louisiana plumbers are licensed by the State Plumbing Board of Louisiana, whose site is spbla.com. It is reasonable to check any plumber's license before hiring, and ours is no exception.

Working with slab homes and older houses

Many homes here sit on a concrete slab, so adding a drain for a new bath can mean cutting the slab. Older houses, including those in the East Main Street Historic District, may have drain pipe that does not match modern fittings, so the plumber looks at what the old pipe is made of before connecting to it. Camera work with sewer inspection is one way to see the existing drain before the project begins.

How an installation project runs

1

Walk the space.

The plumber measures, checks the existing lines and talks through what you want.

2

Price and plan.

You get a written price and a list of what is included and what is not.

3

Rough-in and fixtures.

Pipe goes in before walls close, and fixtures are set after finishes are done.

4

Final test.

Pressure, drains and fixtures are tested, and any inspection is scheduled.

If a fixture is failing instead of being replaced for style, faucet repair or toilet repair may cost less. Water heater changes are on the water heater installation page.
