When a main line backs up for the second time in a month, a snake is no longer the answer. Hydro jetting pushes high pressure water through the line to scour the pipe wall, and it can be arranged on short notice through our 24/7 line. Until the plumber arrives, stop using water and keep the lowest drains in the house clear of belongings.

Hydro jetting uses a hose with a nozzle that fires water backward and sideways while the hose pulls itself forward through the pipe. The spray strips grease, soap scum and mineral scale from the walls and can cut small roots, which is why it succeeds where a cable only opens a narrow channel.

Backups that justify jetting

A cable stalls partway down.

Grease or scale may be coating the line beyond the point the cable reached.

The clog returns within weeks.

A narrow channel through buildup fills back in. A clean wall does not.

Slow drains in the whole house.

A lined, narrowed main restricts every fixture a little instead of one fixture a lot.

A restaurant or shop line that smells.

Grease from food prep builds up in horizontal runs where flow is slow.

What jetting does not fix

Water cannot repair a cracked, collapsed or offset pipe, and aggressive pressure on fragile older pipe can make a weak spot worse. That is why a camera goes into the line first. If the picture shows a break or a belly full of standing water, the right next step is sewer line repair, not more pressure.

Hydro jetting also does not replace maintenance. A kitchen line that carries cooking grease can coat again, and the plumber will tell you how often a recleaning is reasonable for your pipe, based on what the camera showed rather than a fixed schedule.

Jetting after flooding and storm seasons

When Hurricane Ike pushed lakes over their banks and flooded parts of New Iberia in September 2008, drains across the area took on silt and debris. Heavy rain still sends sand and plant material into yard drains and low lines. A jetter flushes that material out, and a camera confirms the line is clear to the main before you close the cleanout.

How a jetting visit runs

1

Camera first.

A small camera shows pipe material, roots, grease and any damage before pressure is used.

2

Choose the nozzle.

A penetrating nozzle opens a stoppage, and a scouring nozzle with rear jets cleans the wall.

3

Jet the line.

The hose works in passes from the cleanout toward the main, with flow checked as it goes.

4

Verify.

A second camera pass shows the clean wall, and you see the result before the plumber leaves.
